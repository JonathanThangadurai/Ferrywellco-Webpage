Scientific foundation

The research we build from.

Our team works with research on fine particulate pollution (PM2.5), heat and cardiovascular health. The evidence tells us why these conditions matter; place, timing and activity help us decide how to present them in a useful way.

01

PM2.5 is more than an air-quality number

PM2.5 means airborne particles with a diameter of 2.5 micrometres or less. These particles can reach deep into the lungs. Evidence reviewed by the World Health Organization links exposure to fine particles with cardiovascular outcomes, including ischaemic heart disease and stroke. That makes PM2.5 relevant when describing the environment in which people are active.

02

Heat is more than air temperature

The body must release heat to maintain a stable internal temperature. Hot air, humidity, sun and physical exertion can make that harder, increasing strain on the cardiovascular system. Duration, shade, wind and access to cooling also change what a person experiences.

03

Why consider heat and PM2.5 together?

An outdoor activity can involve heat and fine particle pollution at the same time. The health effects of each exposure are well studied; the size and circumstances of their combined effect, especially for a specific person or activity, remain research questions. FerryWellCo therefore keeps both signals visible and interprets them in the context of the planned activity.

04

From evidence to useful information

A nowcast estimates current conditions; a forecast describes what may happen next. Placing those estimates beside the location, time and activity makes them more useful. GOTime presents this context for sport and movement. A separate site-operations application would use a site-level analysis and debrief for planned work.
