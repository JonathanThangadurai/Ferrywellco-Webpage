Scientific foundation

The research we build from.

Our team works with research on air pollution, heat and health, while considering other outdoor exposures such as pollen. GOTime uses that foundation to present environmental information beside an activity someone has planned.

01

Air pollution includes particles and gases

PM2.5 describes airborne particles with a diameter of 2.5 micrometres or less. It is one important pollutant, alongside others such as nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) and ground-level ozone (O₃). The World Health Organization links exposure to air pollution with cardiovascular and respiratory outcomes. Different pollutants need to be understood as distinct signals, even when a general air-quality index combines them.

02

Heat is more than air temperature

The body must release heat to maintain a stable internal temperature. Hot air, humidity, sun and physical exertion can make that harder, increasing strain on the cardiovascular system. Duration, shade, wind and access to cooling also change what a person experiences.

03

Why consider conditions together?

An outdoor activity can involve heat and air pollution at the same time. The effects of each exposure are studied, while the size and circumstances of their combined effect for a particular person or activity remain research questions. Our GOTime example keeps heat and PM2.5 visible separately while putting them beside the planned activity.

04

Pollen is another outdoor exposure

Pollen is an airborne allergen rather than an air pollutant. It matters especially to people with allergies, and its relevance can vary by season and location. Wind, humidity and other conditions can also change the experience of being outdoors.

05

Putting the information to use

A nowcast estimates current conditions; a forecast describes what may happen next. GOTime brings those estimates beside the location, time and activity someone has chosen. In an outdoor app, that means the information can appear where the user is already making a plan.
